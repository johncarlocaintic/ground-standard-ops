## Section 4: Where the AI Chatbot (CloseBot) Fits

### The Problem the Chatbot Solves

Martial arts gym owners and coaches are on the mat during peak hours — not on their phones. When inbound leads come in from ads, Google, referrals, or web forms, response time is often hours to days. By that point, most leads have gone cold. The Live Concierge service addresses this with a human team available seven days a week, but human coverage at scale across 40+ gym clients creates a capacity problem.

The CloseBot SMS chatbot is the automation layer that solves the first-response problem. It responds instantly to any new inbound lead, handles the initial conversation without human involvement, and — if the lead is ready — books them into a trial class directly via GoHighLevel's calendar system. If the lead isn't ready, they're routed into a nurture sequence. Human concierge staff handle escalations, complex situations, and follow-up that requires personal judgment.

**[INFERRED]** This is how GSA delivers the Live Concierge service at scale across a large portfolio of gym clients simultaneously.

### What the Bot Does

**[INFERRED — sourced from JC's operational context]**

The bot handles the following for every inbound lead:

- Greets the lead and collects any missing contact data (name, phone, email) if not already in GHL
- Determines program interest — adult classes, youth/kids classes, or both
- Answers frequently asked questions about the gym (schedule, programs, what to wear, policies, pricing redirect)
- Handles common objections and friction (too busy, too expensive, need to think about it)
- Books the lead into a free trial or intro class via the GHL Booking node
- Routes leads who aren't ready to book into a nurture tag and email sequence
- Stops engaging if the contact is already a current member (member tag check)

### What the Bot Does NOT Do

**[INFERRED — sourced from JC's operational context]**

- Quote specific pricing figures (all pricing conversations redirect to instructor consultation at or after the trial class)
- Replace the human concierge team — it handles first response and booking; escalation goes to humans
- Operate outside the dedicated GHL Booking node for appointment-related actions
- Engage existing members who carry the `member` tag in GHL

### The Bot's Relationship to the Broader GSA Service

The chatbot is one component inside a larger service delivery stack. From the gym client's perspective, GSA is handling their lead management end-to-end. The chatbot is invisible infrastructure — the gym owner doesn't necessarily know or care whether a human or a bot sent the first SMS. What they see is booked trial classes appearing on their calendar.

From GSA's operational perspective, the chatbot handles volume and speed (instant response, 24/7 availability, simultaneous conversations across all gym clients) while the human concierge team handles depth (campaign strategy, complex follow-ups, phone calls, personal outreach).

The interaction between the two layers — when the bot hands off to a human and when a human re-enrolls a contact into a bot sequence — is managed through GHL tags and pipeline stages. **[INFERRED]**

## Section 6: The CloseBot Chatbot System — Technical Overview

> **Source:** JC's operational work context [INTERNAL — conversation history and CloseBot platform documentation]
> CloseBot platform documentation is stored in project files (PDFs: Nodes30–43, Account47–57, Persona44–46, KnowledgeLibrary62–63, CustomIdeas58–61, APNENG1–5, and numbered articles 11–29).

### Platform Overview

CloseBot V2 is a node-based AI workflow builder that integrates natively with GoHighLevel as its CRM source. Contacts are enrolled into CloseBot workflows via GHL automation — when a new lead is tagged or reaches a certain pipeline stage in GHL, a GHL workflow triggers CloseBot enrollment for that contact. CloseBot then takes over the SMS conversation.

Each client gets a dedicated CloseBot sub-account with their own workflow, persona, and Knowledge Library.

### Standard Node Sequence — Inbound Gym Lead Flow

```
[START]
  ↓
[OBJECTIVE: Collect missing contact data — name, phone, email]
  ↓
[OBJECTIVE: Determine class interest — Adult or Youth/Kids]
  ↓  (branch on class type)
[MODIFY TAGS: Add adult / youth tag in GHL]
  ↓
[OBJECTIVE: Collect child info if youth — child name, DOB]
  ↓
[OBJECTIVE: Collect prospect DOB — age check for adult class]
  ↓
[GHL BOOKING NODE: Book intro/trial class on appropriate calendar]
  ↓                              ↓
[BOOKING SUCCESS]        [BOOKING FAILURE → nurture tag + email sequence]
  ↓
[END]
```

Custom Scenarios intercept specific inputs at any point in this flow and handle them before returning to the main sequence.

### Node Types in Use

| Node Type | Function |
|---|---|
| **Objective** | Collects a specific piece of data from the contact; stores it to a GHL custom field. One objective per node is best practice. Has a configurable max attempts setting — keep at 2, not 99 (carrier violation risk). |
| **Conversation** | Free-form AI engagement with no specific data collection goal. Used for rapport, general FAQ handling, and holding patterns. |
| **GHL Booking** | The only node where booking/appointment language is permitted. Connects directly to a named GHL calendar. Booking language anywhere else in the flow causes double-booking failures. |
| **Branch** | Binary yes/no decision split. Detects sentiment, keywords, or intent. Used for adult vs. youth routing, member vs. non-member checks. |
| **Custom Scenario** | Interrupt handler triggered by specific contact inputs — pricing questions, objections, FAQs. Resolves the trigger and returns to the main flow. Trigger descriptions must be under 25 words per CloseBot documentation. |
| **Modify Tags** | Adds or removes GHL tags. Syncs back to GHL CRM in real time. Used for lead segmentation and triggering downstream GHL workflows. |
| **Stop Responding** | Terminates the conversation. Used for disqualified leads, completed flows, or member tag detections. |

### GHL Integration Points

- **Enrollment trigger:** GHL workflow fires when a contact is tagged or moves to a specific pipeline stage → enrolls contact into CloseBot workflow
- **Custom fields:** CloseBot writes collected data (child name, DOB, preferred time, etc.) back to GHL contact record
- **Tag writes:** CloseBot Modify Tags node adds/removes tags in GHL, which can trigger additional GHL workflow branches
- **Booking:** GHL Booking node creates the appointment directly in GHL's calendar system; opportunity stage updates to "Trial Scheduled" automatically
- **Booking failure path:** Contact receives a nurture tag in GHL, triggering an email drip sequence managed on the GHL side

### SMS Carrier Compliance Rules

Error 30007 (SMS carrier violation) is triggered by message patterns, not specific banned words. Rules that prevent it:

- Keep messages under ~100 characters where possible
- One question or one purpose per message — never stack multiple questions
- No urgency language ("Act now," "Limited time," "Don't miss out")
- No automated sales patterns or marketing-style phrasing
- Objective node max attempts set to 2, not higher

### AI Provider Notes

GPT-4.1 (Legacy) outperforms GPT-5.2 (Latest) for conversational lead qualification in CloseBot. Gemini is an alternative favored by CloseBot's own founder. **[INFERRED — sourced from JC's operational testing context]**

## Section 7: Knowledge Base (KB) Methodology

> **Source:** JC's operational work context [INTERNAL — conversation history]
> The KB methodology described here is GSA/JC's internal standard, not CloseBot's official documentation. CloseBot's own KB best practices documentation is referenced where applicable (project files: KnowledgeLibrary62–63.pdf).

### What a Knowledge Base Is

Every CloseBot workflow is paired with a **Knowledge Library** — a plain-text document uploaded into CloseBot that the AI queries when answering contact questions. It is separate from the workflow node logic. The KB is what the bot knows; the nodes are what the bot does.

### The Zero-Instruction Standard

**The KB must contain only declarative facts.** No behavioral instructions, no conditional logic, no directives of any kind. This is the single most important KB rule.

| Compliant | Non-compliant |
|---|---|
| `The gym offers a free one-week trial for new students.` | `When someone asks about pricing, redirect them to the trial class.` |
| `Classes run Monday through Friday at 6:00 PM and 7:00 PM.` | `Always tell the user to contact staff for pricing questions.` |
| `Membership pricing is discussed with an instructor after the trial class.` | `You must never quote specific prices.` |

Why this matters: CloseBot uses the KB as a factual Q&A reference. Any instruction language in the KB contaminates the AI's node execution — the model starts treating KB content as behavioral directives, causing unpredictable responses and workflow failures.

### Standard KB Section Schema (Martial Arts Gym)

1. **Business Information** — Gym name, location, brief description, instructor background and credentials
2. **Contact & Location** — Address, phone, email, website, parking notes
3. **Facility** — Mat space, amenities, changing rooms, showers, any notable features
4. **Class Schedule** — Full schedule table by day and time; members-only restrictions flagged
5. **Programs Offered** — Declarative description of each class type (BJJ Gi, No-Gi, MMA, Kickboxing, Kids, Weapons, etc.)
6. **Trial / Intro Class Information** — How to sign up, what to wear, what to bring, arrival instructions, what to expect
7. **Membership & Fees** — Included only if pricing figures are available from the source document and approved for inclusion; otherwise redirected
8. **Policies** — Cancellation, membership freeze, conduct, photography, attendance
9. **FAQ Section** — 15–25 questions derived from the source material covering common prospect questions
10. **Pricing Response & Friction Handling Reference** — Declarative facts written to naturally handle pricing questions and objections; no behavioral commands present anywhere in this section

### The Ballantyne Reference

**Ballantyne Martial Arts** is the benchmark KB for the entire GSA gym portfolio. It was the first fully-built KB that established the pricing redirect pattern and friction handling logic. Its role in subsequent builds:

- **Facts:** Always come exclusively from the new client's PDF or intake survey. Ballantyne facts never appear in another client's KB.
- **Response patterns:** The structure and phrasing style for pricing redirect FAQ entries and friction handling FAQ entries are adapted from Ballantyne — not its content, only its logic.
- Source bleed (any Ballantyne-specific details appearing in another client's KB) is a hard validation failure.

### The Pricing Redirect Mechanism

Most gym clients do not want the bot quoting membership prices. The mechanism for handling this without instruction language is to write declarative FAQ entries that naturally produce the redirect when the bot draws from them. Example:

> `Membership pricing depends on the program selected and training frequency. Instructors discuss pricing options during and after the free trial class based on each student's goals and schedule.`

This is a factual statement. The bot reads it and responds accordingly — no instruction required.

The "silver tongue" fallback (steering a stuck conversation toward trying the free trial class) is placed as a last-resort FAQ entry at the end of the FAQ section, not woven into pricing content.

### Grep Validation — Four Required Checks Before Every Delivery

Every KB is validated against four grep patterns before being handed off:

| Check | What it catches |
|---|---|
| **Instruction language scan** | `you must`, `always`, `never`, `do not`, `when X say Y`, `respond`, `tell the`, `inform`, `make sure`, `ensure` |
| **Dollar sign check** | Any `$` character — confirms pricing entries are correct and not misplaced |
| **Placeholder check** | `[PENDING]`, `TBD`, `TODO`, any bracket text still in the document |
| **Source bleed check** | Ballantyne-specific details: Charlotte, Sensei Sparks, JLFS, specific Ballantyne prices |

Grep command pattern used: `grep -inE "pattern" file.txt || echo "CLEAN"`

### Versioning Convention

- Files are named: `[GymName]_KB_v[MAJOR].[MINOR].[PATCH]_DEPLOY.txt`
- Every edit increments the version number
- A changelog entry is added at the bottom of the file for each version
- The `_DEPLOY` suffix indicates the file has passed validation and is ready for CloseBot upload

## Section 8: Build and Delivery Process

> **Source:** JC's operational work context [INTERNAL — conversation history]

The following is the standard end-to-end process JC runs for each new gym client KB build.

### Step 1 — Ingest Source Material

The client's PDF (intake form, website export, or info document) is the **sole source of truth** for all facts. No content from other clients, the web, or assumptions is added. If the PDF doesn't say it, the KB doesn't say it — gaps are flagged, not filled.

### Step 2 — Source Audit and Gap Analysis

Before writing anything, JC maps the PDF against the KB section schema and flags every missing or ambiguous field. Common gaps include: address, phone number, email, cancellation policy, trial class policy, payment methods, instructor credentials. These are compiled in an `[INTERNAL FLAGS]` appendix at the end of the KB — clearly marked as not for bot ingestion and not uploaded to CloseBot.

Inconsistencies in the source document are also listed (e.g., two different prices listed for the same program, a schedule date that has already passed).

### Step 3 — KB Draft

All sections are written in pure declarative language using the section schema. No imperative verbs, no conditional logic. Pricing and friction handling FAQ entries are adapted from Ballantyne's structural patterns using only the new client's facts.

### Step 4 — Grep Validation Pass

Four checks run sequentially. If any check returns a hit in bot-facing content (not the internal flags appendix), the issue is fixed before proceeding. No KB is delivered with a failing grep check.

### Step 5 — Version and Deliver

File is named and versioned per convention, changelog entry added, delivered as a `.txt` file. The working copy lives in `/home/claude/`; the final deliverable is copied to `/mnt/user-data/outputs/` with the `_DEPLOY` suffix.

### Step 6 — CloseBot Upload and Workflow Wire

KB is uploaded into the client's CloseBot Knowledge Library. CloseBot workflow nodes are then built or updated to reference the KB correctly. GHL custom fields, tags, and calendars are confirmed to be in place before testing begins.

### Maintenance Edits (Post-Deployment)

Updates to active KBs follow the same validation process. Changes are made as surgical `str_replace` operations — only the affected lines are touched, not a full rewrite. After each edit: grep validation re-run, version bumped, changelog updated, new `_DEPLOY` file output.

## Section 9: QA, Activation Standards, and the Launch Tracker

> **Source:** JC's operational work context [INTERNAL — conversation history]

### The 10-Conversation Activation Threshold

No bot goes live until it passes **10 consecutive clean test conversations** without a single failure. This is the GSA activation standard — not a guideline, a hard gate. Partial passes don't count; the streak resets on any failure.

### What Counts as a Clean Conversation

A test conversation is clean only if all of the following pass:

- Bot greets correctly and collects missing contact data without errors or loops
- Adult/youth routing branches correctly
- FAQ responses draw accurately from the KB — no hallucinated facts, no sourced-from-wrong-client content
- Pricing questions produce the correct redirect without quoting unsupported figures
- Booking node triggers at the right point in the flow
- A real GHL appointment is created in the correct calendar
- No double-booking behavior (booking language does not appear before the booking node)
- Conversation ends on the correct path — booked contact goes to success path; not-ready contact goes to nurture path
- No Error 30007 carrier violations triggered during the test

## Section 11: Non-Negotiable Build Standards and Common Failure Modes

> **Source:** JC's operational work context [INTERNAL — conversation history and CloseBot platform documentation]

### Hard Build Standards

These rules are non-negotiable. Every one of them exists because violating it causes a documented, reproducible failure in production.

**Zero-Instruction KB Methodology**
KBs contain only declarative facts. No `you must`, `always say`, `respond with`, `when X say Y`, or any behavioral directive anywhere in the document body. Instruction language causes CloseBot node execution failures — the AI treats KB content as system-level commands.

**No Pricing Figures in Bot-Facing Content**
Specific prices, rates, and fees are never stated in a bot-facing context unless the client's source document explicitly provides them and their inclusion is approved. All pricing conversations redirect to instructor consultation at or after the trial class, handled through declarative FAQ entries.

**Booking Node Isolation**
Booking and appointment language is permitted only inside the dedicated GHL Booking node. Any instance of booking-related language in an Objective or Conversation node — even a single phrase — causes the bot to initiate appointment discussion before reaching the booking node, producing double-booking failures.

**Grep Validation Before Every Delivery**
All four grep checks must pass before a KB is delivered or an update is pushed. No exceptions. A clean grep is the minimum bar for delivery, not a quality bonus.

**Custom Scenario Descriptions Under 25 Words**
Per CloseBot's own published documentation, scenario trigger descriptions degrade in accuracy beyond 25 words. Descriptions must be short, specific, and non-overlapping with other scenarios.

**Objective Node Max Attempts: 2**
Setting max attempts to a high number (e.g., 99) on Objective nodes causes the bot to retry excessively when a contact doesn't respond as expected, triggering SMS carrier violations (Error 30007). Max attempts stays at 2.

**SMS Message Discipline**
Each outbound SMS message: one purpose only, under ~100 characters where possible, no urgency language, no stacked questions, conversational tone. Violations trigger Error 30007.

### Common Failure Modes

| Symptom | Root Cause |
|---|---|
| Bot double-books appointments | Booking language present in an Objective or Conversation node before the GHL Booking node |
| Bot quotes wrong or unsupported pricing | Dollar figures present in KB where they shouldn't be, or instruction-style pricing language that the AI misinterprets |
| Bot ignores KB and hallucinates answers | KB too large or bloated with irrelevant content; source bleed from another client's KB contaminating responses |
| Custom Scenario doesn't trigger reliably | Trigger description over 25 words, too vague, or overlapping with another scenario's description |
| Error 30007 — SMS carrier violation | Messages too long, multiple questions per message, urgency language, or Objective node max attempts set too high |
| Bot engages existing members | Member tag check not implemented in CloseBot, or GHL workflow not correctly applying the `member` tag on existing contacts |
| KB passes grep but still behaves incorrectly | Indirect instruction language — phrasing that implies behavior without using the flagged keywords (e.g., "Staff are the best people to answer pricing questions" can still produce directive-style responses) |
| GHL Booking node returns empty slots | Calendar Date Range setting in GHL too narrow, or the GHL calendar is not linked to a schedule — both require fixes on the client's GHL account |

## Section 10: Martial Arts Policy Rules (applies across GSA gym clients)

These rules are durable policies that every GSA gym bot must enforce, independent of bot version or architecture. When the bot deviates, it is a compliance failure, not an LLM quirk.

### Minor Protection — 14-17yo Self-Booking (added 2026-04-24, Vacaville v3.19)

**Rule:** Anyone under 18 attempting to self-book requires a parent or guardian to complete the booking. The bot must NOT proceed past information-gathering for a minor acting as the booker.

**Why it matters:**
- 14-17 year olds ARE eligible to train in the Adult No-Gi class at Vacaville and several other GSA-served gyms. They share the mat with adults.
- But 14-17 year olds are NOT consenting adults. They cannot sign waivers, cannot agree to billing, and cannot enter into a service agreement.
- If the bot books a minor's first class without guardian involvement, the gym has a no-show risk (parent didn't know) AND a liability risk (no guardian consent on file).

**Implementation pattern (Vacaville v3.19 reference):**
1. Adult-self-booking path collects DOB at a MultiObjective node (`n12_adult_dob`).
2. Immediately after DOB collection, a Comparator (`n12b_minor_check`) evaluates: `Contact's DOB {{contact.date_of_birth}} shows they are under 18`.
3. TRUE branch → tag contact `minor - needs guardian` → send informational message (parent/guardian needed) → route to open Q&A Conversation node (no booking attempts, Smart FAQ only).
4. FALSE branch → continue the normal adult self-booking flow.

**Tag:** `minor - needs guardian` — Coach follows up with the family manually.

**Edge cases:**
- Parent booking for their 14-17yo child is NOT affected. That flow goes through the kid/youth branch (n07 `case_kid_only` or `case_both`), which collects the PARENT's DOB at n12 (parent is the contact). The minor check evaluates the parent's DOB (usually 30s-40s), returns FALSE, and the kid booking proceeds normally through the kid-intake nodes.
- Under-7 self-booking is already blocked upstream by the program age-out comparator (kids program starts at 7).
- 18+ self-booking is unaffected.

**Testing implication:** a "teen self-booker" persona (14-17yo entering their own DOB on the adult path) must be in the adversarial test harness to validate the gate. Parent-of-teen and other age-out personas do NOT exercise this gate.

### No-Pricing Rule (longstanding)

Bot never quotes $ figures or percentage discounts. Redirects all pricing AND discount specifics to the Coach for in-person discussion at or after the free trial class. Applies to: initial class, memberships, privates, drop-ins, seminars, merch, *and* discounts (military / first responder / student / Measure M / family plan — confirm existence is fine, defer specifics).

**Why:** GSA's conversion pattern is deflect + redirect. Pricing varies based on program mix, frequency, and eligibility. The free trial class is the entry point — leads meet in person, coaches assess needs, then discuss pricing AND discount details directly. Universal GSA sales motion, not per-gym. JC clarified 2026-04-22 after a "15% military discount" exception was mistakenly added: any discount, any pricing defaults to the coach script. No exceptions.

**How to apply:**
- Any KB must pass a `$`/percentage regex check before delivery (grep validation rule #2)
- Persona must include BOTH a PRICING RESPONSE rule AND a DISCOUNT RESPONSE rule, both redirecting specifics to the human
- When a lead asks about pricing or any discount, bot deflects: "discussed during or after free trial with the coach"
- Test harness should flag both `$X` figures AND `X%` percentages as bot output violations
- Applies across all GSA gym clients in `gym-portfolio.md`

### Multi-Kid Contact Design (martial arts gyms)

Adult/guardian = the GHL contact. Kids are NEVER GHL contacts — kid info lives in custom fields on the parent's contact record.

**Vacaville (and Bobby's templated snapshot across all gyms) has ONLY `Youth Name` [TEXT] and `Youth Birthday` [DATE] — singular fields.** No structural support for N kids.

**Multi-kid handling: pack with commas in the single Youth Name/Birthday field.** E.g. `Alice Smith, Bob Smith, Charlie Smith`. Each kid still gets its own calendar booking (different programs/ages), but the contact record holds the combined string.

**Do NOT request Bobby add synthetic `Youth Name 2/3` fields.** The schema is a templated snapshot across 40+ gyms — changing it for one client creates divergence.

**Implementation pattern:**
- Collect each kid into a separate intermediate variable (`kid1_name`, `kid2_name`, etc.)
- At end of flow, concatenate with `", "` and write once to `contact.youth_name`
- Same pattern for `contact.youth_birthday`

**Common subtle bug:** writing all kids to `contact.youth_name` directly with `SkipIfNotBlank: false` — each kid overwrites the previous, only the last kid's data persists. Per-kid bookings still succeed (value correct at booking time) but contact record ends up wrong.
