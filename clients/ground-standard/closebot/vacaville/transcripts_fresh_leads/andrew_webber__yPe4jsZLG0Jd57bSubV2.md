Hi Andrew,

Did you know that approximately one quarter of all adults in the United States suffer from major depression? It’s true – and it’s
still a disease that’s often misdiagnosed and misunderstood.

Some of the most common treatments for depression are talk therapy and antidepressant medications. Many people rely on one or both
to help them control their depression. But exercise can play a role, too.

In some of my earlier emails, I mentioned that vigorous exercise releases feel-good hormones called endorphins in your body. Those
endorphins act as mood elevators. In fact, they’re responsible for the phenomenon known as runner’s high.

Exercise isn’t a cure for depression. That would be an irresponsible claim to make. But, it is true that working out regularly can
diminish the symptoms of depression and make you feel better about yourself and the world around you.

I’ve had some students tell me that when they started taking martial arts classes, their depression symptoms abated. As their
self-confidence grew, they became better able to cope with setbacks and emotional disappointments.

Some of the reasons for this are things I’ve already talked about. For example, the practice of mindfulness is something that can
help people learn to manage depression. Likewise, learning self-control and goal-setting can be beneficial.

I’m not suggesting that signing up for a martial arts class will mean you can stop taking anti-depressants or seeing your
therapist. But it is a healthy thing for both your body and mind, and I think you’ll see and feel the difference in your outlook
if you study the martial arts.

Hi Andrew,

I’ve been teaching martial arts for a long time. It always amazes me how many people are misinformed about the nature of martial
arts. In fact, I often get asked this question:

Do I need to enter competitions if I study martial arts?

The short answer is no – but there’s more to it than that. Let’s talk about competitions and sparring so you understand what will
happen if you decide to take a class with us.

The martial arts classes we teach provide students with a great workout. Since many of the moves we teach are blocking moves, you
will need to participate in partnered exercises in class. These include sparring and demonstrations. That’s essential to the
learning process, but our teachers always do whatever they can to keep their students safe.

Testing for belts and stripes occurs on a regular basis. You will need to demonstrate mastery of key moves to move up, and some of
those tests may involve a partner.

You will not be required to enter outside competitions. However, many of our students – including some who started out thinking
they wouldn’t want to compete – decide to do so. They want the challenge of facing an opponent in a formal setting. They want to
test themselves.

You may decide that you like the idea of competing. If you do, we’ll help you prepare for the competition. And, if you don’t ever
want to compete outside of class, that’s fine with us. We can still teach you the skills you need and help you get fit and feel
great about yourself.

The question of whether you compete is yours to answer. We won’t push you to do it if you don’t want to and choosing not to won’t
affect your ability to learn.
